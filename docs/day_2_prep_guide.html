A 10-minute primer: how a PE analyst reads the news 10 min

Day 2's case and the extension sprint are built around a question your finance teammates ask daily: “of everything published today, what actually matters to my portfolio?”

  • A private-equity firm owns a handful of companies (“portfolio companies”) for 3–7 years. Analysts monitor news for signals that change the investment case: a competitor's acquisition, new regulation, a customer bankruptcy, management departures, input-cost shocks.
  • Most news is noise. The valuable skill is triage: tag each story by company, theme and severity, and escalate the few that matter. That tagging job is exactly what Day 2's LLM classifier does.
  • What “good” looks like to an analyst: no missed critical stories (recall on the bad news), tolerable false alarms, and a one-line reason they can check. A system that is 89% accurate but can't say which 11% it gets wrong is unusable.
  • Hold that thought for the sprint rubric: every demo must name what the tool should not be trusted for.
